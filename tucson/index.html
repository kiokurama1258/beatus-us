The first UNESCO City of Gastronomy in the United States.

In December 2015, Tucson became the first city in the U.S. to join the UNESCO Creative Cities Network as a City of Gastronomy. UNESCO's own recognition points to the longest continuous agricultural history of any American city — a 300-year tradition of vineyards, orchards and ranching. More than two dozen food festivals run in Tucson every year, including Tucson Meet Yourself, which draws over 100,000 people annually. That is not a marketing slogan; it is a documented, international food-culture credential few U.S. cities can claim.

The demand backing it up is real too. The University of Arizona had 54,384 students enrolled in fall 2025, and Tucson's accommodation and food services sector generated $2.00 billion in sales in the most recent economic census — a market roughly the same size as Omaha's despite very different populations. Whether it is students, festival-goers or long-time residents, Tucson diners already have a reason to go looking for their next meal.

Ghost kitchens

Ghost Kitchen Marketing: How Delivery-Only Brands Get Found and Reordered

By Bruno Vasconcellos, founder of Beatus Mídias
October 2, 2026 · 6 min read
Direct answer: Ghost kitchen marketing has to replace everything a storefront does for free. With no sign, no foot traffic and no dining room, a delivery-only brand needs strong app listings, paid ads aimed at its exact delivery zone, a direct ordering page so regulars don't cost commission forever and packaging and follow-up that turn first orders into repeat ones.

Why is marketing harder for a ghost kitchen?

A traditional restaurant gets some visibility just by existing: people walk past, see the sign and remember it. A ghost kitchen, or virtual brand, only exists on screens. Customers discover it inside a delivery app or an ad, and if the brand doesn't stand out there, it doesn't exist at all. That makes the delivery apps tempting as the only channel, but every order through them pays commission, including orders from people who already love the food. A ghost kitchen that wants healthy margins needs to use the apps for discovery and build its own path for repeat orders. See the current DoorDash and Uber Eats fees to understand what is at stake.

What should a ghost kitchen do first?

  1. Fix the listing. Clear brand name, appetizing photos of every best seller, short descriptions and a focused menu that is easy to scan.
  2. Build a direct ordering page. The same menu, faster checkout and a reason to order there, as explained in commission-free online ordering.
  3. Run ads in the delivery zone. Meta ads with one dish per video, scheduled before lunch and dinner, sending people to your own page.
  4. Use the packaging. An insert in every bag with a QR code for direct ordering and an offer for the next order.
  5. Follow up. With consent, send a timely message that brings first-time customers back before they forget the brand name.

How do you measure a ghost kitchen's marketing?

Track cost per new customer from ads, the share of orders coming through your own channel, average ticket and how many first-time customers order again within a month. Watch app ratings and reviews closely, because they are your storefront. A live dashboard that shows these numbers by brand and by channel helps you decide where to spend next week instead of guessing.

The bottom line

A ghost kitchen lives or dies by visibility and reorders. Use the apps to be discovered, but build a direct channel and a follow-up system so your best customers keep coming back without a commission on every order.

Frequently asked questions

How do ghost kitchens get customers?

Mostly through delivery apps and paid ads, since there is no storefront or foot traffic. Strong listings with appealing photos and clear menus help inside the apps. Meta and Google ads aimed at the delivery zone bring new customers to a direct ordering page. Packaging inserts and follow-up messages, sent with consent, turn first orders into repeat orders.

Can a ghost kitchen succeed without DoorDash or Uber Eats?

It is possible but harder, because the apps provide discovery that a delivery-only brand otherwise has to buy with ads. Many ghost kitchens use the apps for new customers and build their own ordering channel for repeat orders. The goal is not to leave the apps entirely, but to avoid paying commission on every order from loyal regulars.

Do ghost kitchens need a Google Business Profile?

It depends on how the business operates and whether it meets Google's guidelines for a Business Profile, so check the current guidelines before creating one. Whether or not you have a profile, make sure your brand name, menu and ordering link are easy to find when someone searches for you, and keep the information consistent across apps and your website.

How Much Should a Dental Practice Spend on Marketing?

By Bruno Vasconcellos, founder of Beatus Mídias
October 1, 2026 · 6 min read
Direct answer: A dental marketing budget is easiest to set by working backward: decide how many new patients your schedule can absorb, multiply by what a new patient costs you to acquire, and add management and software costs. Generic percentages of revenue can be a sanity check, but your own cost per new patient and patient value are what tell you how much you can invest profitably.

How do you work the budget backward?

  1. Decide how many new patients per month your team and chairs can absorb.
  2. Estimate your cost per new patient, from your own data if you have it.
  3. Multiply the two to get a starting media budget.
  4. Add agency or staff time, software and creative costs.
  5. Compare the total with what those new patients are worth over time.

How should you split the budget?

Put most of the money where demand already exists, usually Google search for urgent and high-intent treatments, then use Meta for elective care. Keep part of the budget for testing and move money every month toward the treatments and channels with the lowest cost per booked patient.

When should you spend more?

  • When cost per new patient is stable and well below patient value.
  • When the schedule has open capacity you want to fill.
  • When follow-up is fast enough to handle more leads.

Spending more before the follow-up is ready usually raises the cost per patient, because more leads go cold.

The bottom line

Don't copy someone else's budget. Start from your capacity and your cost per new patient, measure every month and invest more only where the numbers show it pays back.

Frequently asked questions

Is there a standard percentage dental practices spend on marketing?

You will find rules of thumb, but they vary by source, market and stage of the practice. A new practice building its patient base usually needs more than an established one with a full schedule. Use percentages only as a sanity check and base the budget on your capacity and cost per new patient.

Should a new dental practice spend more on marketing?

Usually yes, because it has no patient base, few reviews and little search visibility yet. Early spending builds the base that later brings referrals and recall visits. Track cost per new patient from day one so you can reduce paid spend as reviews, SEO and referrals start to carry more of the load.

How often should a dental marketing budget be reviewed?

Monthly for adjustments and quarterly for bigger decisions. Each month, move money toward the treatments and channels with the best cost per booked patient. Each quarter, compare the full cost of marketing with the value of the new patients it brought, and decide whether to grow, hold or cut.

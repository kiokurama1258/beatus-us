Retention

How to Reduce Dental No-Shows and Fill Hygiene Recall

By Bruno Vasconcellos, founder of Beatus Mídias
October 1, 2026 · 5 min read
Direct answer: To reduce dental no-shows, ask patients to confirm, remind them close to the visit, make rescheduling easy and follow up the same day with anyone who missed. Recall works the same way: a list of patients who are due, worked every week with messages that make booking simple. Existing patients are often the cheapest appointments a practice can fill.

Why do patients miss appointments?

Most no-shows aren't rude. People forget, their day changes, they feel anxious about treatment, or rescheduling feels like more trouble than skipping. Each reason has a fix, and none of them requires more ads.

Why patients miss and what fixes it
ReasonFix
ForgotA reminder that asks for a reply, sent at the right time
Schedule changedA one-message way to reschedule
Anxiety or cost worriesA short personal message before the visit
Booked a long time agoA confirmation a few days ahead and a reminder the day before

What is the difference between a reminder and a confirmation?

A reminder informs. A confirmation asks for an answer. When a patient doesn't confirm, your team knows in time to call or to offer the slot to someone on a short-notice list.

What should happen after a no-show?

Message the patient the same day, without blame, with two new times. The longer you wait, the less likely they are to come back.

How do you work the recall list?

  1. Pull the list of patients due or overdue for hygiene every week.
  2. Send a short, friendly message with a booking link or two times to choose from.
  3. Follow up once more after a few days.
  4. Call the ones who don't reply, starting with those overdue the longest.
  5. Track how many recall appointments were booked each week.

Automated texts and emails need the patient's consent, so confirm how your practice collects it before turning on any automation. An AI assistant can handle the replies and offer new times.

The bottom line

No-shows and overdue recall are revenue the practice already earned and then lost. Confirmations, easy rescheduling and a recall list someone works every week often add more appointments than a new campaign.

Frequently asked questions

Should a dental practice charge a no-show fee?

Some practices do, but a fee can create friction with good patients who had a real reason. Many start with better confirmations and easy rescheduling, then apply a fee only to repeated no-shows, clearly explained in advance. Whatever you choose, put the policy in writing and apply it consistently to every patient.

When should dental appointment reminders be sent?

A common pattern is a confirmation request a few days before the visit and a reminder the day before or the morning of the appointment. For visits booked months ahead, such as hygiene recall, add an earlier reminder as well. Test the timing with your own patients and keep every message short.

Can an AI assistant help with recall and no-shows?

Yes. It can send confirmations, answer replies such as "can I come later?", offer new times and flag patients who need a call from the team. It works best connected to your schedule or with a clear handoff to the front desk, and always with the patient's consent to receive messages.

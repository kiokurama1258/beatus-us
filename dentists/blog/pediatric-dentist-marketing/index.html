Pediatric Dentist Marketing: How to Win the Trust of Parents

By Bruno Vasconcellos, founder of Beatus Mídias
October 1, 2026 · 6 min read
Direct answer: Pediatric dentist marketing speaks to parents, not children. Parents choose a pediatric practice on trust, convenience and how their child will feel, so the essentials are a strong Google map listing with recent reviews, search ads for parents looking right now, a reply to every inquiry within minutes and recall reminders, sent with consent, that keep families coming back.

What do parents look for in a pediatric dentist?

Parents usually read reviews from other parents, look at photos of the office, check whether the practice accepts their insurance, look for appointment times outside school hours and try to sense how the team handles anxious kids. A parent who cannot find these answers in a minute usually moves on to the next practice on the map. Most of that is answered before they ever call, on your Google Business Profile and your website. Make sure both show:

  • Real photos of the office, the waiting area and the team.
  • Accurate insurance and payment information.
  • Hours, including early, late or school-break times if you offer them.
  • What the first visit looks like, explained in plain words for parents.
  • An easy way to reach you by text or chat, since many parents prefer it to a phone call.
  • Which ages you see, including teens, if that is part of your practice.

Which ads work for pediatric practices?

Google search ads on terms like "pediatric dentist near me" or "kids dentist accepting new patients" capture parents who need a dentist now. Meta ads can introduce the practice to parents in your area with friendly video of the team and the office. Ad platforms restrict health-based targeting, so aim ads at adults in your service area by location and broad interests. Only feature a child in any ad or post with written permission from a parent, and keep the message about the experience, not about clinical results. State dental boards can regulate dental advertising, so check your board's rules before publishing. Track which campaigns produce booked first visits, not just calls.

How do you keep families for years?

  1. Send recall messages for the next visit, with consent and an easy opt out.
  2. Send reminders before every appointment to cut down on forgotten visits.
  3. Offer to book siblings back to back to save parents a trip.
  4. Ask every family for an honest review after the visit, without incentives.
  5. Make rescheduling easy by text, so a conflict becomes a new date instead of a no-show.
  6. Let an AI assistant answer parents' messages in seconds, day or night, and offer appointment times, so a busy parent never waits for a callback.

The bottom line

Pediatric marketing wins parents with trust and convenience. Show the real practice, answer every message fast, and use consent-based recall and reminders so one new family becomes years of visits and referrals to other parents.

Frequently asked questions

How do pediatric dentists get more new patients?

Most new families find a pediatric practice through Google search, the map and recommendations from other parents. A complete map listing with recent reviews, search ads for parents looking right now and a fast reply to every inquiry cover those doors. Referrals from general dentists and pediatricians can add more, when the practice keeps those relationships warm.

Can a pediatric dental practice show children in its ads?

Only with written permission from a parent or guardian, and even then it is wise to keep it simple: a happy visit, the office, the team. Avoid clinical claims, since state dental boards can regulate dental advertising. Many practices rely mostly on the team, the space and parent testimonials shared with consent.

Should pediatric practices text parents?

Many parents prefer text to phone calls, so texting is usually welcome when it is done with consent. Use it for appointment reminders, recall messages and quick answers about scheduling or insurance, always with an easy way to opt out. Keep health details out of text messages that could appear on a lock screen.

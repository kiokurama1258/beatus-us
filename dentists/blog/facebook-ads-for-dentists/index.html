Meta Ads

Facebook and Instagram Ads for Dentists: When They Work

By Bruno Vasconcellos, founder of Beatus Mídias
October 1, 2026 · 6 min read
Direct answer: Facebook and Instagram ads for dentists work best for elective and high-value care that people postpone: aligners, whitening, veneers, implants. The ads create the interest, and a fast follow-up turns it into a consult. They work poorly for urgent needs, where Google search wins, and they fail when leads wait hours for a reply.

Which treatments fit Meta ads?

Treatments people want but put off: straighter teeth, a brighter smile, replacing a missing tooth. Seeing a friendly team or a real patient story can move someone from "someday" to "let me ask". A routine cleaning rarely needs that push, and an emergency belongs on Google.

What creative works for a dental practice?

  • Your real team and office, not stock photos.
  • The dentist explaining a treatment in plain language, in a short video.
  • Patient stories and results only with written consent and within your state's advertising rules.
  • A clear first step: a consult, an evaluation or a question answered by message.

Which health ad rules apply on Meta?

Meta does not allow ads that assert or imply personal attributes, including health conditions, so copy that speaks directly to a person's condition can be rejected. Write about the treatment and the outcome instead of about the reader. Meta has also added limits on how some health-related data can be used for targeting and measurement, so check your tracking setup against its current rules.

Instant forms or messages?

Instant forms collect contact details inside Facebook or Instagram with very little effort, which usually means more leads and more people who forget they asked. Ads that open a conversation or send people to a page with a booking option tend to bring fewer but warmer leads. Either way, the first reply should come within minutes.

The bottom line

Meta ads create demand for elective dentistry. Pair them with honest creative, compliant copy and a reply in minutes, and they become a steady source of consults.

Frequently asked questions

Do Facebook ads work for general dentistry?

They can, especially for new patient offers in a tight area around the practice, but they usually work best for elective treatments. For someone who needs a dentist today, Google search is more direct. Many practices use Meta for aligners, cosmetic care and implant awareness, and Google for urgent and new patient demand.

Can I show before-and-after photos in dental ads?

Only with the patient's written consent and within the advertising rules of your state dental board. Meta also restricts certain before-and-after imagery, so check its current policy before running them. Results should be real, typical of what you deliver and never edited to look better than they were. When in doubt, ask your board or attorney.

How fast should a practice reply to a Facebook lead?

Within minutes if possible. A person who filled out a form while scrolling forgets about it quickly, and the practice that answers first usually gets the conversation. An AI assistant or a clear staff routine that answers right away, including evenings and weekends, keeps those leads from going cold.

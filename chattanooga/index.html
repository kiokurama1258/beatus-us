A city whose restaurants just landed on the map of American fine dining.

On November 11, 2025, four Chattanooga restaurants — Little Coyote, Easy Bistro & Bar, Main Street Meats and The Rosecomb — were named in the inaugural MICHELIN Guide American South 2025, the first-ever regional Michelin guide to cover the US South (Alabama, Louisiana, Mississippi, North Carolina, South Carolina, Tennessee and Atlanta). Little Coyote received the Bib Gourmand distinction; the other three earned a Recommended listing, out of 228 restaurants selected across the whole region. Chattanooga Tourism Co. CEO Barry White called it validation "of what locals have known for years: that our city offers exceptional dining experiences, worthy of international recognition."

Chattanooga itself is growing too — the city's population reached an estimated 194,144 in 2025, up 6.9% from its 2020 Census base of 181,099. The wider metro area grew from 568,306 residents in 2021 to 594,530 in 2025, a pace a University of Tennessee at Chattanooga white paper describes as "more than double the national pace," with the region expected to surpass 600,000 residents soon. UTC itself just posted a record fall 2025 enrollment of 12,060 students, and the city's accommodation and food services sector generated roughly $1.25 billion in sales in the latest economic census. People are moving in, studying downtown, and eating out — the restaurants that show up in that decision win.
